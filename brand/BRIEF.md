# CARSON — Brand Brief

Working record of decisions. Single source of truth for the site build.
Last updated: 2026-08-25

---

## 1. Name

**CARSON.** Not OxyCarson. Not Carson Overseas.

- Parent firm: **Carson Overseas** (registered; rights held to "Carson Overseas" and "OxyCarson")
- "OxyCarson" was a sub-brand of the firm. Dropped — the `Oxy` prefix cheapens the name.
- "Overseas" dropped — reads logistics/trade, not luxury.
- The consumer-facing brand is the single word **CARSON**.

> ⚠️ **OPEN — trademark.** Rights are held for *Carson Overseas* and *OxyCarson*, **not** for
> *Carson* standalone. "Carson" is a common surname and Class 25 (footwear) is crowded.
> This needs a clearance search before any money goes into signage, boxes, or ads.
> Fallback lockups if standalone is blocked: `CARSON 1999`, `CARSON ATELIER`, `HOUSE OF CARSON`,
> or a stylised wordmark registered as a device mark.

## 2. Positioning

- **Tier:** Elevated contemporary. Roughly $150–320 / ₹12,000–26,000.
- **Register:** Maison-grade presentation (Versace-tier production values) with an identity that is
  our own — not a pastiche of any existing house.
- **Hard constraint from founder:** *"It should look like a product, not an art project."*
  Over-designed fashion sites become galleries and stop selling. The product stays the hero.
  Beauty in service of the shoe, never in front of it.

## 3. The story — LEAD WITH THIS

Twenty-five years manufacturing and exporting footwear from Agra to Europe and Indian retail.
Carson is that workshop stepping out from behind its clients and putting its own name on the box.

The line to build the brand on:

> **For twenty-five years, our work left in someone else's box.**

This is the single strongest differentiator against every DTC competitor, and it is entirely true.
Nearly every new premium sneaker brand is a marketing team that found a factory.
Carson is the factory. That is the whole argument.

Second-order beats:
- Not a startup that found a factory. A factory that became a brand.
- Made to a European export standard because that is the only standard we have ever worked to.
- No middle layer. The people who make it, sell it.

## 4. Partners — DECISION: do not name them at launch

Real history (founder-supplied): **bugatti** (European export, ongoing), **Red Chief** (long-standing),
**Metro** (long-standing), **Tata / Zudio** (ended), one further international name unconfirmed.

**Decision: imply, do not name.** Reasons, in order of weight:

1. **Legal.** Manufacturing agreements routinely carry confidentiality clauses, and using a client's
   trademark to promote your own competing brand invites a cease-and-desist. Unverified.
2. **Positioning.** The list is mixed-tier. Naming Zudio next to a $300 sneaker doesn't add
   credibility, it caps the price we can charge. A mixed list averages downward.
3. **Power.** "You have probably already worn our work" is stronger than any list. The reader
   fills the gap with the best name they can think of. Naming closes that door.

Approved on-site phrasing — verifiable, no trademarks used:

> Twenty-five years supplying footwear brands across Europe and India.
> You have probably already worn our work.

**Revisit if:** written permission is obtained from bugatti (the only name that helps the tier).
Then, and only then, a single line — never a logo wall.

## 5. Origin — DECISION: stated plainly, exactly once

**Made in Agra, India.** Stated as fact in the craft section and in the footer. Nowhere else.

- No apology, no hedging, no "Made in India" softening.
- No Taj Mahal, no marigolds, no tourism imagery, no heritage-of-the-subcontinent essay.
- Agra is the largest footwear manufacturing city in India. To anyone in the trade it is a
  credential, exactly as Northampton or the Marche is. To everyone else it is a place name
  under a beautiful shoe.

Rationale for the founder's hesitation: the nervousness is real but it is answered by *treatment*,
not by *concealment*. Buyers at this price ask where shoes are made, and discovering a hidden
answer is far more damaging than reading a plain one. Confidence is the entire trick — a fact
stated without flinching reads as a fact worth stating. Hiding it is the only version that
actually costs credibility.

## 7. Construction claims — what we may actually say

Founder-verified as true:
- **Full-grain leather** on the top of the range
- **Hand-finishing / hand-stitching on part of the upper** — Agra's tradition; machine does the
  bulk, human hands do a defined part

**Copy rule.** Say *"hand-finished"* and *"partly hand-stitched"*. Do **not** say *"handmade"* or
*"entirely hand-stitched"* — not true at scale, and indefensible when a customer asks.

> ⚠️ **UNVERIFIED — must confirm before it ships:** Goodyear welted? Vegetable-tanned?
> Tannery source? Neither has been confirmed. Do not put either word on the site until it is.
