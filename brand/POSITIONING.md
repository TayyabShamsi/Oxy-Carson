# CARSON — Brand Positioning

The single most important document here. Everything else — the site, the
Instagram grid, the box, the price — is downstream of this.

Last updated: 2026-08-25

---

## 1. Your idea, answered straight

> *"Carson is leather. Carson is quality. The leather flips and becomes quality,
> so we associate the brand directly with quality."*

**The mechanic is good. The word is wrong.**

Taking them separately, because they are two different ideas wearing one coat.

### The mechanic — keep it

Associating a brand with one attribute, repeated until the association becomes
automatic, is real positioning strategy and it is how most durable brands were
built. Volvo did it with safety. That instinct is sound and you arrived at it
on your own.

### The word — change it

"Quality" is the single weakest word available to you, for three reasons:

**1. Every competitor already says it.** Read ten footwear brands' About pages.
All ten claim quality. A claim that everyone makes carries no information, so
the customer's brain discards it before it registers. You cannot own a word that
is already in everyone else's mouth.

**2. It is unprovable at the moment of purchase.** Someone looking at a screen
cannot verify quality. They can only verify *reasons to believe* it. Telling
them "quality" asks them to take your word for it — and they have no reason to,
because they have never heard of you.

**3. Announcing it is anti-luxury.** This is the part that matters most for
where you want to sit. Luxury brands never tell you they are good. Hermès does
not have a page explaining that its bags are well made. The confidence to *not*
say it is precisely the signal. A brand that insists on its own quality sounds
like it is arguing with someone.

### What to own instead

> **Carson is the maker.**

Not a brand that commissions shoes. The workshop itself, under its own name.

This works where "quality" fails:

| Test | "Quality" | "The maker" |
|---|---|---|
| Can a competitor claim it? | Everyone does | Almost none can — they don't own factories |
| Provable? | No | Yes: 1995, SA-8000, own tanneries, Italian design partner |
| Ownable? | No | Yes |
| Produces "quality" in the customer's head? | Asks for it | **Yes, as their own conclusion** |

That last row is the whole argument. If someone learns you have made shoes for
thirty years, source your own hides, design with an Italian house and are
audited to SA-8000, **they conclude quality themselves** — and a conclusion a
person reaches on their own is held far more firmly than one they were handed.

You get the association you wanted. You just don't ask for it.

## 2. The positioning statement

> For people who have learned to read a shoe before they read a label,
> **Carson is leather footwear sold by the workshop that makes it** — thirty
> years of European export manufacture, Italian design and our own hides, now
> under our own name, at the price that is left when nobody stands in between.

**The line that carries it:**

> **Made by the people who make it.**

**The line that proves it:**

> For thirty years, our work left in someone else's box.

**The line that closes it:**

> You have probably already worn our work.

That third one is the most powerful sentence you have. It is true, it cannot be
disproved, and it makes the reader do the work — they fill the gap with the best
brand they can think of. It is stronger than naming a client would be, and it
carries no legal risk. Use it sparingly so it keeps its force.

## 3. Where Carson sits

|  | Position |
|---|---|
| **Category** | Premium leather footwear |
| **Price** | £180–340 / ₹19,000–36,000 |
| **Against mass premium** (Clarks, Aldo, Charles & Keith) | We are not a shoe with a name on it. We are the workshop. |
| **Against DTC challengers** (Oliver Cabell, Koio, Beckett Simonon) | They found a factory. We are one. Thirty years, audited, our own leather. |
| **Against heritage houses** (Church's, Grenson, Loake) | The same standard, without three decades of retail margin priced in. |
| **Against luxury houses** (Ferragamo, Tod's) | We do not ask you to pay for the name. That is the whole point. |

**The enemy is not another brand. It is the markup.** Position against the
invisible middle layer, never against a named competitor.

---

## 4. What we say, in order

Order matters more than content. This is the sequence:

1. **Made by the people who make it.** — the position
2. **Since 1995.** — the proof of time
3. **Italian design, our own hides, finished by hand in Agra.** — the proof of method
4. **SA-8000 audited.** — the proof somebody else signed
5. **£265.** — the reward for having read this far

Never lead with price. Never lead with "quality". Never lead with Agra.

## 5. Voice

Carson speaks like a person who knows the trade and has no need to impress you.

**Do:**
- Short declaratives. "We are the factory."
- Admit the limits. *"Most of the work is machine work, and we will not pretend otherwise."* This one sentence buys more trust than a page of adjectives, because a brand that admits one thing is believed about everything else.
- Specifics over adjectives. "Kolkata and Chennai" beats "finest".
- Say the number. 1995. 35,000 pairs a month. SA-8000.

**Don't:**
- "Luxury", "premium", "exquisite", "curated", "timeless", "elevated", "crafted with passion"
- Exclamation marks
- Any claim you cannot defend if a customer emails and asks
- Explaining that you are good

**The test:** would a person who has made shoes for thirty years say this out
loud without wincing? If not, cut it.

## 6. On the "leather becomes quality" animation

Don't build it as written — it dramatises the weak word. But there is a version
worth building, and it is better:

**"Thirty years. Four names. One box."** — a sequence where a shoe crosses the
black bar and the label on the box changes, three times, before settling on
CARSON.

It says the same thing your idea wanted to say, except it dramatises the *true,
ownable* claim instead of the generic one, and it is a story rather than a
statement. Nobody else can run that animation, because it isn't true of them.

*(The current site already carries the restrained version of this: the product
crossing the black bar. Build the sequence only if the site needs a bigger
moment later — do not add motion for its own sake.)*

## 7. Proof assets, ranked by strength

1. **The Italian design association** — reframes Agra from compromise to
   sophistication. Strongest single asset. Confirm what may be said.
2. **SA-8000** — third-party audited. Almost no competitor at this price has it.
3. **1995** — a date beats every adjective.
4. **Own tannery sourcing** — vertical integration; explains the price.
5. **The client history** — powerful, but *implied only*. See `BRIEF.md` §5.

---

## 8. The one-sentence test

If someone asks a Carson customer "what's Carson?", the answer we want is:

> **"They're the factory. They made shoes for other brands for thirty years and
> now they sell their own."**

Not "they're a luxury shoe brand." Not "they're good quality." If the customer
repeats the factory line, the positioning has landed.
